**yes, i want to discuss it, now i want you to explain all the fields in the char sheet and how they behave in the final ymap when fed to seedance2**

Here's the whole anatomy, and what each field actually becomes when the clip is emitted.

First, the mechanics. A char sheet isn't itself prompt text — it's a home. When you link a sheet into a clip, the app drops that character into the clip's character_modeling block, keyed by char_name, with each filled field as a sub-key. So the Imp linked into a clip emits roughly:

character_modeling:
  The Imp:
    base: "…"
    features: "…"
    physics: "…"

Fields you leave empty don't emit as blanks — they just aren't there. And the sheet's bound image (@image2) joins the clip's references automatically when you link, so you don't attach it by hand.

Where that block sits in the YAML matters. It comes after title, reference_handling, style, visual_feel and duration, and before the storyboard. That order is deliberate: the model reads how to treat your references, then the visual language, then who's in the scene, then what happens. Character identity is established before any action asks the model to move it.

Now the seven fields.

base is the identity line — what this thing is in one breath, and crucially which reference it corresponds to. This is where role-binding lives: "matches @image2 — take the face, fur and garment; do not use its background or pose." That's the single highest-leverage sentence on the sheet, because unlabeled references get averaged into hybrids, and a turnaround ref with a white void behind it will absolutely try to put your imp in a white void. Short. Identity plus ref job plus ref exclusion.

features is the render spec — everything the model needs to draw it correctly, in nouns and materials rather than adjectives. Colors by name, textures by material, wardrobe by item. This is the longest field and the one that fights identity drift hardest, because specificity is what the model locks onto. "Blue imp" drifts; "slate-blue skin under shaggy blue-black fur, tall black tufted cat ears above thin grey elf ears, flat amber eyes with slit pupils" doesn't. Anything you don't name here, the model invents fresh on every generation.

physics is how it moves and how its materials behave — the field most people skip and shouldn't. It's not choreography (that's the beats); it's the standing rules of this body. Weight, gait, what leads a turn, how fur and cloth lag behind motion, how it holds stillness. The model applies this underneath whatever the beats ask for, so one good physics field makes every shot in the project move consistently without you re-specifying.

Those three are the permanent core and belong on the sheet.

emotional_state is the demeanor carried through the clip — the register the performance plays in. Slightly different job from the others: it colors expression and timing rather than geometry. Keep it about visible behavior. "Holds a stare too long, then drops it all at once" renders; "conflicted about his past" doesn't.

Then the three scene fields, which are the interesting ones because they exist to be filled per clip, not once:

movement_in_this_scene restricts or shapes movement for this shot only, and it sits inside the character block on purpose. Putting a movement rule here rather than in the avoid: list at the bottom is measurably more effective — the model parses it alongside the character description instead of as a detached constraint. This is where "the imp never stands fully upright in this scene" goes.

state_in_this_scene is the character's condition at this clip's start. Wounds, missing pieces, soaked fur, torn pelt, posture. Two reasons it matters: the model has zero memory between generations, so damage from the previous clip resets unless you re-describe it — and a damaged character is more unique than a clean one, so it drifts back toward the reference less. By clip five of anything with stakes, this field should be doing real work.

power_this_scene is the mechanical behavior of an ability that fires in this clip — what it looks like, how it moves, what it does to the environment. Only when something activates.

The reason those last three live on the sheet as available fields but should be left empty here: the sheet resolves live into every clip that links it. A scene rule written on the sheet leaks into all of them. Write them on the clip.

One more thing worth knowing — if you render a clip through Omni Flash instead of Seedance, none of this emits as YAML. It gets rewritten as natural prose and the @ tags translate automatically. Same fields, same discipline, different surface.
